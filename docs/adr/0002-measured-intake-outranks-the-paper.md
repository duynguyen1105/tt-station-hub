# The Barem-measured intake outranks both the handwritten barem and the delivery note

A biên bản carries two claims about how much fuel arrived: the litres the supplier
says they sent (the delivery note quantity), and the litres a worker copied out of
the printed Barem for the measured height. We compute the litres ourselves from
Trường Thịnh's Barem — **SL barem** from the reviewed height, and **Nhập vào hầm**
as barem(after) − barem(before) — and treat that as the value, because it is the
only one of the three that is a measurement of the tank rather than a claim about
it. The other two are kept and displayed beside it: the delivery note as the
comparison, the handwritten figure as a red flag when it disagrees by 1 L or more.

## Consequences

- Short deliveries, spillage and under-filled compartments become visible as a gap
  between the measured intake and the delivery note. Before this, the two were
  equal by construction, because the note's quantity _was_ the recorded intake.
- The litres booked to tồn kho are now the tank's, so they can differ from the
  supplier's invoice. That difference is the point, but it is a change in what the
  books mean and the accountant should be told once.
- The reviewer can still overtype every computed value; the paper biên bản is the
  legal record and the human confirming it has the last word.
- A non-positive difference is never auto-filled — zero means the tank took
  nothing, and a negative means the tank fell during the delivery, which section
  (d) of the biên bản exists to catch. Filling either would launder an anomaly into
  a number.
